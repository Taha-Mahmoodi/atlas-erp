MRP has **no natural key and none was invented**: re-planning a date is legitimate, it posts to neither ledger, and D-049 regeneration deletes prior un-firmed proposals, so a reclaim costs one discardable planning document. Two genuinely double-posted and are guarded: **ticket depletion** skips ingredients already issued for the ticket — per **ITEM**, not per ticket, because `job_payloads` chunks a large check into several jobs over disjoint ingredient sets and a document-level flag would have silently dropped every chunk after the first (the plan's suggested "does this ticket already have issue moves?" is exactly that bug) — implemented in **inventory's** handler where the duplicate is born rather than in hospitality's service, so any future republisher is covered too; and **bank-statement import** returns the statement its own job created, keyed on the `import_job_id` the router already stamps (no new column) with a partial UNIQUE index as the DB backstop. **Task 2, the sweeper** (`core/job_sweeper.py`, migration 0049): startup + 5-minute lifespan loop, PENDING reclaimed after 10 min and RUNNING after 2 h (nothing tells "dead" from "slow" but elapsed time), 50 reclaims per tick so a post-outage backlog cannot schedule thousands of tasks at once, and a 3-attempt ceiling on a new `core_jobs.attempts` column that marks a permanently-failing job FAILED and leaves it there. `_run_handler` now CLAIMS its row with a conditional PENDING→RUNNING update and returns if it loses — without that, reclaiming a row whose original task was merely queued behind `MAX_CONCURRENT_JOBS` would run the handler twice concurrently. - [done] P0 review fixes — the sweeper's two design errors (2026-08-15) Adversarial review of PR #200 landed two DESIGN findings, both fixed rather than documented around. **(1) The attempt ceiling counted sweeps, not executions.** `attempts` is only ever incremented by the sweeper, and a handler that actually raises is set FAILED by `_run_handler` and never swept again — so `MAX_JOB_ATTEMPTS` could not fire on "fails, is reclaimed, fails again" at all.
